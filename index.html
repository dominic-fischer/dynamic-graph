About this visualization

Nodes

Each circle represents a person, whereby size indicates importance based on correspondence volume in the Bullinger Corpus. It is proportional to the number of letters sent and received. Note that the maximum size is clamped to the second largest node, as Bullinger himself is an outlier in correspondence volume. When many persons are shown at once, all circles shrink by the same factor so that everybody fits on the screen.

Geographic colour mapping

Color indicates the main location in a year, i.e., where the most correspondence was exchanged. A place's colours are based on its coordinates, whereby:

  • Direction from Zürich → hue (color family)
  • Distance from Zürich → saturation (strength of color)

Edges

Edges show relationships active during the selected year. Their thickness is the number of greetings/letters in that year. Each edge is split into strands, one per direction and kind, each as thick as its share, with a white arrow pointing where it goes. Hover over an edge to see the counts.

Greetings

Every greeting has an intermediary: the person whose letter carries it, or who is asked to pass it on. Greetings are used to infer teams, and drawn in the direction they travel:

  • Blue, type I, greetings passed on to somebody: the writer asks the addressee to greet a third person, who is considered part of the addressee's team. Arrow: addressee → greeted person.
  • Green, type II, greetings sent from somebody: the writer forwards greetings from a third person, who is considered part of the writer's team. Arrow: greeting person → writer.

Letters

Letters are drawn as grey rows of arrows ( -->--> ) from the writer to the addressee. Where a greeting edge and a letter edge join the same two persons, they are drawn next to each other. By default only letters to or from Bullinger are shown (see ⚙ settings).

Time

The slider filters the network to a specific year.

The play button animates the historical evolution.

Filtering

The graph shows the persons who appear in the greeting data in the selected year, plus Heinrich Bullinger, who is always shown. All their greeting edges are always shown.

Letter edges come in two groups, each with its own checkbox: between greeting persons (both persons appear in the greeting data of that year; on by default) and all others. Ticking all others also adds the persons who only appear in letters.

The Show slider selects a percentage of these persons, starting with those with the most letters in the corpus (ties are broken by activity in that year).

Interaction

  • Hover a person: highlight the person, their edges and everyone they are connected to
  • Drag: move nodes
  • Click a person: open biography
  • Hover an edge: its greetings ("X greets Z (via Y)") or letters
  • Click an edge: full list of its greetings or letters
  • Toggles: letter edges between greeting persons, and all other letter edges
  • ⚙ Settings: by default only letters to or from Bullinger are shown; untick to include letters between other persons
